     Continuous variants of the ULDM and GW scenes

     Context

     Every scene in animation_scripts/aion.py is stop-start: draw_legs plays a
     leg
     while the atoms move, then fire_pulse / fire_vertical_pulse plays a pulse
     in
     several separate scene.play calls while the atoms are frozen. That is right
     for
     the explainers — ClockPhase, MachZehnder, SinglePhotonMachZehnder,
     Gradiometer, SingleLaserKick, LargeMomentumTransfer all keep it.

     It is wrong for the three scenes with something passing through them. All
     three are
     space-time diagrams — x is lab time, y is position on the baseline — so
     freezing
     the atoms freezes lab time itself. In DarkMatterPhase that is a visible
     defect: the
     now tracker added last session drives the field trace marker, the modulated
     level
     diagram and the detuning, and it currently sits still for the whole 2-3 s
     of every
     pulse while the field is supposed to be oscillating. In GradiometerGW the
     strain
     trace and the atoms stop together while a photon crawls up the baseline
     twelve times
     slower than the diagram says it should.

     The fix is one lab-time ValueTracker per scene whose value is the diagram's
     x
     coordinate, driving atoms, trails, photon worldlines, trace markers and
     phase hands
     together. Nothing can then be inconsistent with anything else, because
     everything is
     a function of the same number. The atoms land on their vertices exactly
     when the
     pulses cross them, by construction.

     The existing scenes stay; the continuous ones are new, separately
     renderable classes.

     Decisions taken

     - One constant rate, V, throughout. No dilation, no slow-motion. Lab time
       runs
       at the speed it already runs at during a leg, and keeps running through
       the pulses.
     - Pulses strobe. At V a photon crosses the baseline in GR_LAG / V = 0.21 s
       and
       the full traversal from the laser line to the top of frame takes ~0.49 s,
       against
       2.45 s today. The continuous variants accept that: the pulses have
       already been
       explained by the stop-start scenes, and these ones are about what is
       passing through.
     - Scope: DarkMatterPhase, GradiometerGW, GradiometerGWStretch. The quiet
       Gradiometer stays stop-start.
     - Expect each scene to run ~14 s instead of ~30 s.

     The one rule

     Between the first pulse and the last, every scene.play must advance the
     clock.
     A play that does not is exactly the freeze these variants exist to remove.
     That rules
     out the current standalone beats — play(FadeIn(dots[k])) 0.3 s,
     play(FadeIn(bands[k]))
     0.5 s, the 0.5 s state_colors swap at the mirror — each of which has to be
     folded
     into a clock-advancing play or replaced by an always_redraw that switches
     itself on
     as lab time passes it.

     Changes

     animation_scripts/style.py

     One small block in the pacing section near V / PULSE_SPEED
     (style.py:78-83),
     commented in the file's existing voice: what running lab time at V costs,
     and
     PULSE_HOLD — the lab time a strobed vertical column is held before it fades
     (~0.4 units, i.e. 0.2 s at V).

     animation_scripts/aion.py — shared machinery

     Move leg_point (aion.py:1365) and wavy_path (aion.py:1376) up into a new
     # --- running the diagram continuously --- block placed after
     fire_vertical_pulse
     (aion.py:551), so every continuous helper sits above its first use. Pure
     moves, no
     edits. draw_wavy_legs (aion.py:1386) stays where it is — it is the
     stop-start
     GW-stretch mover and is untouched.

     leg_point with a zero displacement reduces to straight interpolation, so
     these two
     existing helpers serve the bowed GradiometerGWStretch legs and the straight
     legs
     in GradiometerGW and DarkMatterPhase. That is the reuse the whole design
     rests on.

     New in that block:

     - run_to(scene, clock, to, *anims, **kwargs) — the only primitive. Plays
       clock.animate.set_value(to) alongside anims with rate_func=linear and
       run_time=(to - clock.get_value()) / V.
     - worldline(corners, disp=None) → a function from lab time to a point,
       piecewise
       over the corners an arm turns at, clamped at both ends so an arm can be
       added
       before it exists and left parked after it recombines. Built on leg_point.
     - carry(atom, path, clock) — attaches the position updater. Must not be
       used
       with draw_wavy_legs, whose teardown calls atom.clear_updaters()
       (aion.py:1424)
       and would wipe it.
     - growing_trail(corners, color, clock, disp=None) → a VGroup of one
       always_redraw per leg, each wavy_path(..., s_max=clamped progress). One
       per leg
       rather than a polyline per arm, because that is what wavy_path's s_max
       already
       does and it keeps the bowed and straight cases identical.
     - climbing_pulse(clock, x_at_lower, slope, z_from, z_to, z_at) →
       always_redraw of
       the photon's worldline drawn as far up the baseline as it has got, from
       photon_worldline's geometry (aion.py:92).
     - sweep_pulse(scene, clock, x_at_lower, near, far, lag=...,
       reference=False, note=None, z_near=..., z_far=..., quiet_x=None) —
       fire_pulse's continuous counterpart, same
       arguments where they carry over. It splits the sweep at the two crossing
       instants so
       each Flash is launched at the right lab time, and returns what it drew
       instead
       of fading it (see below).
     - strobe_pulse(scene, clock, x, top, flash_at, hold=PULSE_HOLD) — the
       vertical
       counterpart. The ClockPhase geometry has no light travel time, so the
       column is not
       something that climbs: it appears whole at the crossing and fades over
       hold of lab
       time while the atoms carry on through it. Keeps the existing rule that
       the column
       stops at the topmost atom it acts on (aion.py:545-549), using the known
       vertex.

     Two details that fall out of the constant rate:

     - Flash the vertex, not the atom. Flash snapshots get_center() when handed
       a
       Mobject (verified in the installed manim 0.21: Flash.__init__ does
       self.point = point.get_center()), so on a moving atom the ring is left
       behind. It
       also accepts a bare point — and the vertices are already computed
       analytically
       (gw_vertices, aion.py:1355), so passing the vertex is exact and marks the
       event
       rather than chasing the mobject.
     - Worldlines, ghosts and notes persist until the next pulse fires. Today
       fire_pulse fades all of them 0.3 s after the pulse passes. At V that is
       under
       half a second on screen, and arrival_note ("late", "early") and the "no
       wave"
       label are the explanatory payload of GradiometerGW — unreadable if they
       go with
       the pulse. sweep_pulse returns them; the caller fades them in the play
       that fires
       the next pulse, giving each note the full leg (~1.9 s) to be read.

     animation_scripts/aion.py — scene structure

     Each of the three base classes gets its pulse/leg sequence extracted into
     an
     overridable fly(), leaving the shared setup (frame, trace, dials, atoms,
     hands) and
     the shared readout (sweeps, ports, signal) in construct. This is the idiom
     the file
     already uses — ClockPhaseTerms(ClockPhase) (aion.py:838) overrides a
     phase_budget
     hook on the same pattern. The geometry bundle (vertices, atoms, phase
     trackers, rate,
     excited, big, dots) is stashed on self in construct.

     The seams are clean in all three. In DarkMatterPhase, fly() spans
     aion.py:1158
     (the first trace dot) to aion.py:1211 (the last pulse) and must stash the
     arm atoms
     and their hands on self, since the readout at aion.py:1217 removes them. In
     the
     two gradiometer scenes fly() spans the first FadeIn(dots[0]) to the last
     fire_pulse (aion.py:1495-1575 and 1724-1781).

     fly() also takes ownership of the beats that differ: it plays
     FadeIn(dots[k]) in
     the stop-start version, and in the continuous version adds an always_redraw
     that
     switches each trace dot on as lab time passes it. Same for
     DarkMatterPhase's field
     bands, which in continuous mode grow with the clock — the wash fills in
     behind the leg
     as it is flown (guard the Rectangle width against zero at the start).

     - GradiometerGWContinuous(GradiometerGW)
     - GradiometerGWStretchContinuous(GradiometerGWStretch)
     - DarkMatterPhaseContinuous(DarkMatterPhase)

       (aion.py:1541-1543, 1566-1568, 1180-1187) with updaters off the clock:
       each
       arm's tracker is rate * (time that arm has been excited up to now),
       clamped to its
       own window. DarkMatterPhase reuses dm_phase (aion.py:909) with the upper
       limit
       clamped to now. Strictly better than the current form — the hands now
       also turn
       correctly through the pulses, which today they do not.
     - Recolour instantly at the mirror, do not animate it. state_colors
       (atom_interferometry.py:179) is atom[0].animate.set_fill(...), and
       manim's
       _MethodAnimation interpolates the submobject's points from a starting
       copy as
       well as its fill. Updaters run after animation interpolation each frame
       (Scene.update_to_time: animation.interpolate(alpha) then
       self.update_mobjects(dt)),
       so the parent's position updater wins on the group's centre — but the
       body would be
       teleported back each frame before the group is recentred, so the
       highlight
       submobject can visibly slide off the body. At strobe pace an instant
       recolour at the
       mirror flash is the right look anyway. state_colors stays for the
       stop-start path.
     - Seed → arm swap at the first pulse (aion.py:1511-1515, 1728-1732,
       1160-1167) is already an un-animated remove/add; in continuous mode add
       the arm
       atoms with their updaters already attached so they land exactly where the
       seed was.
     - draw_ports (aion.py:588) gets an extra=() passthrough forwarded to its
       internal draw_legs (aion.py:630) — one line, backwards compatible, and
       the only
       change to a helper the stop-start scenes share. The continuous scenes
       pass
       clock.animate.set_value(...) so lab time rides along with the port
       flight; the
       internal dx / V and the clock advance then agree exactly. This matters
       most in

● Added, and derived from the same model the scenes animate rather than quoted.

  GW (GradiometerGW, GradiometerGWStretch, both continuous variants) — the
  signal group now reads:

  ΔΦ = Φ_upper − Φ_lower = 2n (ω_A L / c) h sin²(ω_GW T / 2)

  ∝ L and ∝ n are legible straight off it. The sin² is the resonance — it equals
  1 when ω_GW T = π, which is exactly the case the scenes draw (GW_PERIOD =
  2·GR_T).

  ULDM (DarkMatterPhase and its continuous variant) — two lines in the bottom
  strip:

  Φ = (4ε ω_A/ω_φ) sin²(ω_φ T/2) sin(ω_φ T + θ)
  ΔΦ ≃ 4ε (ω_A L / c) sin²(ω_φ T/2)  (two clouds, L apart)

  One thing worth flagging: DarkMatterPhase draws a single interferometer, so
  the quantity it animates is Φ, not ΔΦ — there's no L in it. The L scaling you
  asked about enters only in the gradiometer, where two clouds sample the same
  oscillation L/c out of step, which is the second line. That's also the nice
  parallel: both signals come out as ω_A L/c × (fractional perturbation) × sin²,
  with h for the wave and ε for the field.

  Verification. I checked all three expressions numerically against the exact
  integrals the code uses (2000 random parameter draws, agreement to 1e-9): the
  ULDM Φ against ω_A integrated over each excited window as dm_phase does it;
  the GW ΔΦ against pulse arrivals delayed by (d/c)(1 + h/2·cos ω t_k) as
  gw_arrival_lags does it; and the ULDM gradiometer differential against the
  ω_φL/c ≪ 1 envelope.

● Shortened to proportionalities, and the two cases now read as the same form:

  GW (GradiometerGW, GradiometerGWStretch, both continuous variants) — one line,
  replacing the two-line equation:

  ΔΦ = Φ_upper − Φ_lower ∝ n L h sin²(ω_GW T/2)

  ULDM (DarkMatterPhase + continuous) — replacing the two full expressions:

  ΔΦ ∝ n L ε sin²(ω_φ T/2)
  in a gradiometer: the wave's form, with ε for h

  The prefactor that came out is 2 ω_A/c, fixed the moment the atom is chosen,
  so dropping it into the ∝ costs nothing and leaves exactly the knobs:
  baseline, photon kicks, interrogation time. T enters as a resonance rather
  than a power — peak at ω T = π (the case both scenes draw), falling as T² well
  below it — which is why I kept the sin² rather than flattening it to ∝ T².
